Bible study for Linux

Read and study the Bible in depth.

Two translations side by side, the Greek and Hebrew under every word, and how the church read each verse. Free, with no account, all on your own computer.

Download for Linux Version 1.7.3 · Flatpak

This is live. Click a word for its Strong’s entry, or a verse number for the passages that echo it and what the church wrote on it.

Everything for close study, on one screen

The Greek word by word

Open an interlinear beside any Bible and read the Greek New Testament or the Hebrew Bible one word at a time, with the gloss, the parsing and the Strong’s number under each. The variant readings and the Hebrew Ketiv are a click away.

The church on every verse

Beside any passage, Voices of the Church gathers what the fathers, the medieval teachers and the Reformers wrote about it: 87,995 quotations from Irenaeus to Calvin, each filed under the verse it explains and dated by its century.

Art for the passage you are reading

Scripture in Art sets paintings, engravings, icons and stained glass beside the verse on your screen: 1,256 images, from Caravaggio’s Calling of Saint Matthew to Doré’s engravings, each with its source and credit.

Reading mode

Clear everything but the text. Seven papers, from white to black, each come with their own ink, and the drop cap is set in gold.

Notes that follow the verse

Highlights, notes and tags show in every translation.

Search and concordance

Every place a Greek or Hebrew word is used.

340,000 cross-references

From OpenBible.info, five times the Treasury’s count.

Maps and places

Maps of the journeys, and photographs of the places a verse names.

Scripture in Stone

Archaeology tied to its verses.

The Book of Generations

The Bible’s family lists drawn as charts.

Listen

Any chapter of the Berean Standard Bible, read aloud.

Reading plans and Today

The day’s reading and the church’s calendar.

Commentaries and confessions

Matthew Henry, Calvin, the 1689 Confession and more.

For those who teach

Write the sermon beside the text, then preach it. Your manuscripts, your notes on the passage and a view for the projector live in one place.

The writing room

Draft in plain text with headings, lists and quotations. Scripture you collect lands in the sermon with its reference, and nothing is lost when you close the window.

Series and dates

Group sermons into a series and keep the days each was preached.

Present

F5 puts a passage on a projector in large type.

Print and export

A passage as a study handout, with your notes and highlights.

A verse as an image

Save or copy a verse set on paper, ready to share.

Get Scriptura

Install it in three steps

  1. 1

    Download the installer

    A small file named scriptura.flatpakref.

    Download for Linux
  2. 2

    Open the file

    GNOME Software or KDE Discover installs Scriptura and keeps it up to date.

  3. 3

    Choose a starting library

    The first screen asks your language, then offers three places to start. You can add or remove anything later.

    Just reading1 BibleAbout 8 MB
    Reading + study4 Bibles · commentary · dictionary · lexicon · cross-referencesRecommended · About 60 MB
    Full library7 Bibles · 3 commentaries · dictionary · lexicon · cross-referencesAbout 74 MB

Or in a terminal:

flatpak install https://andresmessina-sdg.github.io/scriptura-flatpak/scriptura.flatpakref

Scriptura needs Flatpak. Fedora Workstation, Linux Mint, Pop!_OS and Zorin OS include it. On Ubuntu, Debian and others, set it up first.

Colophon

Scriptura is made by Andres Messina and released under the GPL-3.0. This page is set in Noto Serif for Scripture, Newsreader for prose and Adwaita Sans for everything else, the same faces the app uses, and it loads nothing from any other site.

The letters in the margins are the Hebrew and Greek alphabets written out in order, as on the app’s Today page, with an older Paleo-Hebrew letter among them now and then. They stand in the proportion the Bible is written in, counted verse by verse: 73.6% Hebrew, 25.6% Greek and 0.9% Aramaic.
